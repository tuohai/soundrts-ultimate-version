Introduction:
	During the early 1930’s, Germany, under the dictatorial leadership of Adolf Hitler, began a ‘peaceful’ expansion campaign. While building up his military and creating the “Anschluss”, a union between Austria and Germany (against the rules set down by the Treaty of Versailles), Hitler militarized the Rhineland, made a non-aggression pact with Russia, and captured Czechoslovakia. British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain decided not to interfere and risk causing another war so soon after the “War to End All Wars”, and instituted a policy of appeasement and pacifism. Emboldened by the rest of Europe’s lack of response to his aggression, Hitler decided the time was right to invade the rest of Europe. On September 1, 1939, Germany unleashed a blitzkrieg (lightning war) attack on an unprepared Poland. German Panzers and mechanized infantry roared past the futile defenses, and Poland fell in 26 days.
Britain and France declared war on Germany, but did nothing for seven months. The next April, Germany invaded and conquered Denmark, Norway, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and Belgium. Again, the blitzkrieg tactics proved unstoppable. By early May, Germany had swept around France’s poorly placed Maginot defensive line and had begun a blitz deep into the French homeland. Hitler’s conquest of Europe was completed by the end of May, where what was left of the French army and the British Expeditionary Force was hastily evacuated from Dunkirk.
The four month long Battle of Britain was the next step, in which Hermann Goering attempted to smash British morale by daily and nightly bombing raids. Though many British cities were devastated, the Luftwaffe was also nearly destroyed by the brave pilots of the RAF, and by the early warning given by the newly invented RADAR system. It was of these pilots that the new Prime Minister Winston Churchill said “Never in the field of human conflict have so many owed so much to so few”.
From then until June of 1944, no real ground action took place in the west. During the meantime, the Battle of the Atlantic pitted the German navy against the combined naval forces of England and America, which had joined the war after the attack on Pearl Harbor by Japan, a German ally.
Nothing important happened in Western Europe until June 6, 1944. On D-Day, American, British, Free French, and Canadian forces under the command of Dwight D Eisenhower landed on the beaches of Normandy on the northern coast of France. Though heavy casualties were taken, the Allies punched through Hitler’s heavily fortified “Atlantic Wall”, and established a toehold that would eventually lead to Germany’s defeat.
On the Eastern front, Hitler had decided to break his non-aggression pact with Stalin on August 23, 1941. The Germans again used blitz tactics to push deep into Russia. However, supply lines were stretched dangerously and vulnerably long, and a fierce winter dealt severe damage to the German army. The battle of Stalingrad was the worst fighting on the Eastern front, and both sides took horrible casualties. The soviets alone took over 500,000 dead or wounded. During the battle of Kursk, the greatest tank battle in history, the Soviets lost over 5000 tanks.
Knowing he was being squeezed from both sides, Hitler decided to make a final last-gasp counterattack to stop the Allied advance from France. In December 1944, the Germans started the Battle of the Bulge. Though the Allied lines fell back, they never broke, and all territory was regained by January 1945 as the German tanks literally ran out of gas.
With his forces retreating on all sides, and Russian and Allied troops knocking on Berlin’s door, Hitler committed suicide with his new wife on April 30. The war in Europe ended on May 7, bringing to a close the greatest conflict in European history.





What this TC is and is not:
This is a Total Conversion of Westwood’s Red Alert 2. It completely changes the original game, leaving almost nothing leftover. It is basically a very involved game mod. It is distributed as freeware, and the makers ask for no compensation. It is not a glorification of war, and it is not a worship of the Nazis. Any representations of Nazis (SS troops, swastikas etc) are for historical reasons. Denying something existed or repressing the knowledge that it existed doesn’t make it disappear. This mod is not sponsored by Westwood (at this point) and they will be unable to help you with any problems you encounter. The Blitz forum has a support section (http://forums.westwoodnation.com/index.php?act=SF&s=85273d0d2edecd00d67a3a4c4fb34b65&f=39) just for this purpose. This mod was made for fun, not absolute realism, and gameplay reflects that. You post any concerns you have on the forum, but please, no flames.



Changes made:
	Blitzkrieg’s economy is based heavily on Oil Derricks. These buildable oil-pumping stations extract natural crude oil from the ground and refine it into usable and resalable gasoline to fuel the war machines and pay for new equipment, training, and structures. Also buildable are ore refineries. These large structures deploy a slow but fairly well armored transport truck that will seek out the nearest ore mine where ore should be available for pickup. Not all maps have these ore mines, and sometime the ore mines can be very far away from your base making ore truck routes vulnerable.
	Also all new is the game’s music. Some of this music dates from WW2 and some is more recent but done in WW2 style. No RA2 or YR music is to be found. Enjoy!
	Blitz uses unit classifications to distinguish some tanks from others and to make quick decisions easier to make. There are several new kinds. Scout units are small, fast, and usually carry a small cannon or heavy machinegun. They often carry passengers too. Light tanks are like heavy scouts. With either a heavy machinegun or light cannon, these small tanks are better armored and nearly as fast as a scout unit, although they can’t carry passengers. These are the largest units you can get in Infantry war. Medium tanks are slower, more powerful and more heavily armored than light tanks, and often have the best “bang for the buck” of any tank, and can be devastating near the beginning of a game. Once the battle has advanced to heavy tanks though, they are very easily destroyed and are only effective in great numbers. Heavy tanks are the most powerful basic tanks you can buy. Slow, expensive, but massively armored and powerful, you should always fear when you have a squad of them coming towards your base. Tank destroyers are more specialized tanks. Usually turretless, these large tanks have high-velocity anti-armor cannons that can rip tanks to shreds in seconds, though are usually a little less effective versus structures. The better ones are also quite expensive. Artillery are very long range turretless tanks that are best suited for destroying armor and infantry concentrations at long range and destroying enemy structures. Their light armor and large minimum range make them easy targets in a firefight, so be sure to keep them back away from heavy combat. Anti-Air vehicles are just what you would expect. They are armed with either a small flak cannon or several large-caliber machineguns that are effective versus aircraft and infantry.
Blitz has three game modes: Battle, Naval War, and Infantry War. Battles take place inland and are fought with tanks, infantry and aircraft. Naval wars take place on maps made of mostly water with no direct land route to an enemy’s base besides via the sea. Infantry wars place emphasis on infantry, as light tanks are the biggest available armor.
	To add your own music, you need to take the songs you want and convert them to the proper format using the XCC IMA ADPCM WAV Writer, available at http://xccu.sourceforge.net/. Once you use the wav writer plugin through WinAmp and have the new songs in your directory, you’ll need to extract the thememd.ini from your game files (expandmd98) in the RA2 directory using the XCC mixer. Once that is extracted into your RA2 directory and is made readable if necessary, (copy and paste it into word, then recopy the fixed text and paste it back into the original, overwriting the illegible stuff) you can add new music. Follow the examples in the thememd.ini and create your own entries, and make sure that you add the entries to the list at the top. Once all this is done, you should be able to play your own music, though the names etc will be odd (MISSING:THEME:YADDA014 pr something similar) in the options menu. This is only a graphical problem, caused by a lack of matching string file for the thememd.ini. To create the new strings, go back into XCC mixer and launch the XCC String Table Editor under the Launch menu, and select the Red Alert 2 YR option. Once there, find THEME in the list in the box on the left side (You’ll have to scroll over one place likely). From there, it’s just a matter of copying the names you game to the songs in the thememd.ini (Most Blitz songs are along the lines of theme:mt###). Once you have them inserted, use the edit button and put the desired name to be displayed in the large middle box that pops up. Repeat for all songs. This will make the songs’ names appear properly in the options menu. It’s a fair bit of work, granted, but for the tinkerer with time on his hands, its good practice for later modding. Recommended songs: Mettalica – Blitzkrieg, Seek and Destroy, and Orion. Rammstein – Eskimos and Egypt, Asche zu Asche. Also, sound track music works very well. If you have access to it, the music for the US in C&C Generals fits very well. You’ll just need a bit of imagination to come up with names for them.

Allies - The Allied powers consisted of mainly Britain and later, the United States. Though the Free French helped, they were not their own army. After France had fallen, England was the only European nation left free from Hitler’s domination. On December 7, 1941, the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor prompted the Americans to take up the fight as an Allied power, and with American help, the British were able to free France and eventually crush the German war machine.

Infantry:
Rifle infantry – Rifle troops carry an M1 Garrand rifle for use against other infantry.

Marines – These swimming infantry carry sub-machineguns and grenades.

Heavy machinegunner – These troops can set up sandbags and deploy a large machinegun.

Sniper – These soldiers are long-ranged and excellent infantry-killers.

PIAT infantry – These troops carry short-range spring-loaded armor-piercing bomb launchers for use against tanks.

SAS operative – SAS troops carry a pistol and explosive charges.

Medic – These men in their white outfits can heal injured infantry.

Spy – These infantry can disguise and give you good things depending on which building he infiltrates

Engineer – These troops can capture structures and repair bridges.

Train Engineer – These specially trained soldiers allow you to commandeer trains for your war effort. Send them into locomotives to take control.

Dog – Dogs can kill infantry quickly and can identify spies.
 

Vehicles:
Scout Jeep – These famous ATVs are capable of transporting three men, who can fire out of the open-topped back. Though fast, they are vulnerable to all fire.

Scout Greyhound – Small and fast, the Greyhound is a personnel carrying armored car. Its small cannon is useless against actual tanks, but can protect it from other light vehicles. 

Light Mk4 – These tiny tankettes are fast and have a 37mm cannon. Useful for scouting or escorting high-speed transports, these tanks are very ineffective even against Medium tanks unless in large numbers.

Light Stuart – This small, fast tank carries a 37mm gun and heavy machineguns. Useful against infantry and for hit-and runs on ore transport trucks and unguarded artillery etc.

Medium Valentine – This British medium tank is armed with both a 75mm cannon and a machinegun. A good all-purpose tank, it saw lots of service in Africa.

Medium Sherman – The Sherman was one of the most famous tanks of the war. Armed with both a machinegun and a 75mm gun, the Sherman relied on superior numbers and high speed to achieve victory as their armor and armament were less than impressive.

Heavy Churchill – The British heavy tank, the Churchill was armed with both a 90mm cannon and a flamethrower for use against infantry and structures.

Heavy Pershing – The American heavy tank was bigger, stronger, faster, more powerful, and much better looking than the Churchill. Though it arrived too late in the war to alter the outcome much, it 90mm gun was deadly to most German and Russian tanks.

Antitank Wolverine – The Wolverine was the Allied tank-hunter. Armed with a 76mm high-velocity antitank cannon, its high speed made it an excellent hit-and-run tank. 

Artillery Bishop - The bishop mobile artillery tank was used for long-range bombardment of enemy forces and bases. It was slow, lightly armored, and carried a 105mm howitzer. En masse, these self-propelled guns could devastate enemy positions and armor formations.

M16 Halftrack – This high-speed halftrack carried a quad-mount antiaircraft machinegun for use against low-flying planes and light ground-based targets.

Aircraft
Typhoon – These British planes carry rockets for use against armor and ships. They were deadly to the slower German heavy tanks that carried little armor on top.

Avro Lancaster – Lancaster bombers attack in level bombing runs, and are devastating to bases and industry. Their high explosive bombs do little more than give tank crews headaches though.

Spitfire – Automatically launched from an airfield, Spitfires will attack any enemy that gets too close for comfort.

Transport – Transport planes can carry lots of infantry and can paradrop them on command.


Special Structures: 
Industrial plant - this large structure allows allied forces to produce units more quickly and cheaply, aiding their already strong economy.

Nuke Command Bunker – This heavily reinforced bunker is where Allied commanders can call for a nuclear strike. When given permission, the Allied commander can call in a high-altitude B-29 Superfortress to drop a single plutonium powered “Fat Man” nuclear bomb on any given target.

Russians – The Soviet Union first entered the war as a German ‘ally’ by helping capture Poland as per the Non-aggression pact. However, during the late summer of 1941, German tanks and planes roared into the Russian heartland breaking the treaty and forcing the Soviet Union to ‘switch sides’. Though Russian technology, workmanship, and morale were inferior to the Germans, they had a huge wealth of manpower and production facilities set up by Stalin before the war started. In an incredible show, the Russian were actually able to “pack up” their factories and move them to the Urals; out of range of German bombers and closer to natural resources. These factories then began producing incredible numbers of tanks to be used on the front. The most famous of these tanks, the T-34 was almost a match for a German Tiger tank, and a dozen could be built in the time it took to build a single German heavy. With the massive army it built, Russia was able to overrun the German forces in the bitter winter of 1943 and became unstoppable as it raced towards Berlin.

Infantry
Rifle infantry – Though poorly equipped, Russian rifle infantry were effective against other infantry.

SMG infantry – These soldiers carry machine pistols and grenades

Heavy machinegunner – Deploys to use a tripod machinegun

Sniper – Excellent long-range infantry killer, they are useless against armor.

Partisan – These Russian resistance fighters carry explosives and can capture vehicles. They can also disguise themselves.

Engineer –Engineers can capture enemy structures and rebuild bridges.

Train Engineer – These troops can capture trains found on the battle-map and turn them to the Soviet cause. Send them into locomotives to capture the trains.

Spy – These infantry can disguise and give you good things depending on which building he infiltrates

Dog – Dogs can kill infantry quickly and can identify spies.

Vehicles
Scout BA-64 – These armored cars carried infantry and a turret-mounted machinegun.

Light T-38 – Though small, the high speed and heavy machinegun on the T-38 make them excellent infantry-killers.

Light T-70 – The T-70 was armed with a 37mm cannon for use against light armor. They were obsolete at the wars start, and almost none survived the war.

Medium BT-5 – BT-5’s were the best fast tanks the Russian had until the development of the T-34. Weak armor allowed for high speed, but a good gun makes it an excellent early-strike tank or straggler-hunter. 

Medium T-34 – Arguably the best tank of WW2 for its price, the T-34/76 was fast, heavily armored, inexpensive, and armed with a powerful 76mm cannon. Later models (T-34/85) sported an 85mm gun.

Heavy KV-1 – The KV-1 was big, slow, and very powerful. When first seen at the start of Operation Barbarossa, the Germans had nothing that could counter it. One KV-1 reportedly held up the entire German advance for several days when it placed itself on a road bordered by impassible swamps and refused to be destroyed by anything the Germans threw at it, until an 88mm antitank gun was secretly set up behind it at point blank range. Even then, it took several hits to penetrate its armor.

Heavy IS-2 – Though slow, the IS-2 was Russia’s most powerful tank. Its long-barreled 128mm gun was devastating to any and all targets, and its armor could repel all but the heaviest antitank fire. They saw little action because of their late entry into the war, but they were proven very deadly. They were the “lightest” of all the heavy tanks of the war because their armor was only put in select spots keeping weight down.

Antitank SU-85 – These monstrous tank-killers carried a high-velocity 85mm cannon mounted in their turretless hull. This cannon could destroy anything that came within range. The tank’s low profile made it a difficult target to see and hit, and when it was hit, the thick sloped armor could usually deflect most shots.

Artillery Katiusha – The Kat was a simple truck with a simple set of rails on top for launching simple rockets at their enemies. They were the most devastating ground-based mobile artillery weapon of the war.

Anti-air Flak track – These halftracks carried two flak guns for use against ground and air targets.

Support Minelayer – These weaponless vehicles were used to sow minefields to hinder, annoy and kill the enemy.

Aircraft
IL-2 assault bomber – These small planes carry several 500lb bombs for use against soft targets.

IL-4 level bomber – These larger planes carried heavier bombs and more of them. 

Yak attack plane – The Yakamov was the tank-buster of choice for most Russian pilots. These planes automatically attack any target that gets within the patrol range of their airfield.

Transport – These simple Russian transports carry lots of infantry and can paradrop them anywhere on the map.

Special Structures: 
Advanced barracks - This structure allows the Soviets to train two soldiers for the price and time it takes to normally train one.

Germans – Germany, under the leadership of Hitler’s Third Reich, rapidly became a powerful military nation near the end of the Great Depression. After stressing Britain’s pacifism to the breaking point by blitzing Poland, Hitler’s armies and air forces were able to easily overrun all of Europe within a year. After the disastrous Battle of Britain and the failed invasion of Russia, Germany went on the defensive. When Operation Overlord was successfully carried out on Normandy in 1944, Germany knew it was doomed. However, the Fuhrer refused to surrender and the war lasted for almost another year. Though the most technologically advanced nation at the time, German factories had been bombed and resources captured. Germany surrendered on April 7, 1945, and Nazism was abolished.

Infantry
Rifle Infantry – Armed with a Mauser rifle, the well-trained and loyal German foot soldier is the best long-range infantry killer next to the sniper.

SMG infantry – These soldiers have a sub-machinegun and antitank grenades.

Heavy machinegunner – These troops deploy their large tripod machineguns to attack enemy infantry and light vehicles.

Panzerfaust infantry – These troops carry bazooka-like antitank rocket launchers. They are most accurate when deployed.

Sharpfshütze – These specially trained troops carry long range sniper rifles for killing enemy infantry.

Engineer –Engineers can capture enemy structures and rebuild bridges.

Train Engineer – These troops can capture and use trains found on the map. Send them into the locomotive to commandeer the whole train.

Dog – These loyal German shepherds are excellent infantry killers and can also sniff out spies.

SS Sturmtruppen – The Stormtroopers were elite nazi fighters. Better armed and trained than their SMG counterparts, they also carry explosive charges.

SS Rifle infantry – Better armed and trained than the basic soldier, these loyal troops can also destroy buildings and bridges with their explosives.

Spy – These infantry can disguise and give you good things depending on which building he infiltrates

Vehicles
Scout BMW – The BMW motorcycle with sidecar was the most popular form of personal transport in the German army. They carry a machinegun, but are too lightly armored to engage anything much more powerful than a mass of civilians. (Which they did on occasion)

Light Panzer I –The Panzer I was an excellent pre-war tankette used to kill exposed infantry. It was the first successful tank Germany produced, but was painfully obsolete by war’s end. Many were converted to command vehicles.

Light Panzer 38 – These heavier light tanks sported a small anti armor cannon instead of a machinegun. They were not of German design, they were Czech vehicles until the nation was overrun and its factories put to German use.

Medium Panzer IV – The Panzer IV was the second medium tank mass produced during the German military buildup. The Panzer III was useful early in the war, but the IV replaced it in the assault role after 1941. It was a good reliable tank and made its mark in history during the Barbarossa blitzkriegs.

Medium Panther – The Panzer V was the second medium tank that the Germans designed and mass produced. It was an excellent tank and could be built twice as quickly as a Tiger, even though it wasn’t much less effective. Designed by Porsche, it was a fast tank and could go almost anywhere. It gives credit to the T-34 as many features found on the Panther, like sloped armor, were first pioneered in that Russian vehicle.

Heavy Tiger – The Panzer VI, or Tiger, was the most deadly tank in all of Europe for most of the war. They saw most of their action on the Eastern front however, against the hordes of Russian T-34s. Tigers were, however, very complex and expensive to build, and had a poor fuel economy. Their 88mm cannons however were deadly to all Allied tanks, and most Russian tanks that they fought.

Heavy Tiger II – The Königstiger was the most powerful non-experimental tank of the war hands down. Even more powerful and better armored than the dreaded Tiger, the Royal Tiger was impervious to almost everything the Allies could throw at them. Due to their high cost and complexity, only 250 were produced before the war ended.

Antitank Sturm III – The StuG III was one of the earliest German tank hunters. First designed as an infantry support artillery vehicle based on the obsolete Pz III’s excellent chassis, they soon proved well suited to destroying enemy tanks. Their turretless design was the basis of all German tank hunters to follow. Small, moderately fast, and with a low silhouette, they were deadly ambushers against light and medium tanks throughout the war. 

Antitank Elefant – Also known as the Ferdinand, the Elefant was the best tank killer in the German arsenal during the epic Battle of Kursk. They were first deployed without a defensive machinegun, which proved to be an expensive mistake. These odd-looking mobile antitank guns were deadly in open fields, but they proved easy targets in city fighting, where infantry could lob grenades through the open top and back from within buildings and rubble.

   Artillery Hummel – The Hummel, or Bumblebee, was the German field artillery piece of choice. Effective against structures and infantry, tanks were dreaded by the hummel crews. They also lacked a defensive machinegun, making them vulnerable to infantry with grenades or rocket launchers.

APC Stummel – This halftrack APC carried four soldiers that could fire their weapons out of the open top even while the APC was in motion. This fire was complemented by the Stummel’s own heavy machinegun.

Antiair Flak Track – The German anti-air flak track was armed with an 88mm multipurpose flak cannon.

Support Minelayer – The German minelayer was designed to place mines right where the enemy would want them least.

Aircraft
Stuka – These screaming dive-bombers were the most terrifying things that enemy troops had ever seen. With four 500lb bombs, they were effective against all targets due to their accuracy, and their wing-mounted “dive sirens” created an unearthly shrieking that terrified even the most hardened soldiers. They proved slow and unmaneuverable in air combat however, and were obsolete as fighters at the war’s start. As close support planes however, they were unrivaled for much of the war.

Junkers 88 – These level bombers were made famous during the Battle of Britain. They proved resilient to fighter attack, but losses were still heavy. They attack in level bombing runs that are deadly to infantry and can obliterate most structures.

FW-109 – The Focke-Wulf was the best German fighter of the war, and possibly of any side. These will automatically intercept any enemy that strays within their airfield’s range of influence.

Transport – German transports carry lots of infantry and can paradrop them anywhere over the map.

Special Structures:
V-2 launch pad – This German super weapon launches a large ballistic missile at its target, destroying or severely damaging it.

Naval Vessels:
Submarines – These stealthy ship-destroyers were devastating against cruisers, carriers, battleships, and merchant shipping alike, but are vulnerable to groups of destroyers.

Destroyers – These small ships are excellent for scouting and hunting submarines, but are of little use in surface combat due to their small-caliber guns. However, their small guns are very accurate, and large numbers of them can overwhelm and sink larger ships unable to hit the fast Destroyers with their larger, more inaccurate guns. Russian destroyers are armed with Katyusha rocket batteries instead of guns.

Cruisers – These are larger naval artillery ships with 8” artillery cannons and antiaircraft flak weapons. 

Battleships – Only possessed by the Allies and Germans, these huge vessels have 15” and 16” bombardment cannons. These giant ships are devastating to anything that gets within their massive range. They can also defend themselves from air attack with AA guns. 

Carriers – These large warships are only possessed by the allies. They don’t directly attack enemy ships and structures, but rather serve as a floating base for three plane squadrons of F4U Corsair attack planes. Armed with rockets and machineguns, these planes are deadly to enemy ships, structures, tanks and infantry. If a squadron is destroyed, another squadron will be readied and take their place in the air.

Landing Craft – These can also be used to transport tanks and troops across expanses of water to take a land fight to the enemy. Just beware of submarines.


Shared Structures:
Construction Yard – Deep in this underground bunker is a large construction facility that builds all base structures. Defend it well, as it is irreplaceable.

Power Plant – This structure produces the electricity needed to operate radar facilities, power Flak targeting devices, and power the construction centers.

Oil Derrick – These crude oil pumps extract and refine oil to fund and fuel the player’s war efforts. If destroyed, they explode violently, often taking out nearby structures and units. They have build limits based on which side is using them.

Barracks: – These facilities are where all infantry units are trained and deployed.

War Factory – This is where all vehicles are produced. The more existing, the faster vehicles are built. Germans can build two, Allies three, and the Russians four.

Air Command – This structure allows for the command of aircraft and deployment of paratroops for Russia or parabombs for Germany and the Allies. Each air command center comes with a small airfield that will automatically launch three planes to intercept encroaching enemies. If destroyed, the aviation fuel stored within will explode violently.

Bomber Command – This is where aircraft are produced, land, and are rearmed and repaired.

Repair Pad – This structure lets you perform in-field maintenance on friendly vehicles, and allows the construction of tech centers.

Tech center – This is where plans and machinery for advanced units are stored.

Shipyard – During a naval war, this is where naval reinforcements come into the field of battle


Static Defenses:
Guard tower – These tall structures can recognize disguised infantry and will attack all ground targets with a heavy machinegun. Effective versus light tanks and infantry, they won’t even scratch heavier tanks.

Pillbox – These anti-infantry bunkers are much harder to destroy than guard towers, though they will still do no damage to heavier tanks.

Cannon turret – These fixed antitank guns are deadly against smaller tanks and can hold off heavy tanks until mobile support arrives. Don’t count on them to hold the fort alone.

Flak cannon – These 88mm antiaircraft guns fire rapid bursts of flak at passing enemy aircraft. They are useless alone, but can take out groups of planes when en masse.

Bunker – These garrisonable buildings allow the player to create customized defenses for any attack coming his way. Any infantry unit can enter and fire his weapons out of the many gun ports.

Maginot Cannon – These large defensive structures aren’t buildable, but can be found on many maps. They can be captured with an engineer and used against enemies as long as there is sufficient power to operate the aiming and loading machinery. These guns are very powerful and can devastate an attack before they can get close enough to respond.

Trains:

You cannot remove any unit from the train once you put it inside. If the car is destroyed the units will go along with it. The exception to this rule is the train locomotive, you may load and unload units from that car.

The train self heals (slowly though) the only other way to heal the train is to capture the mechanic shop and that will heal it as well. Repair vehicles will not heal it.

Once the train locomotive is destroyed, the rest of the train is destroyed. And if one car is destroyed, the rest of the cars will blow up as well due to fuel line fires. The locomotive has one-way valves to stop such occurrences from destroying it.

-Suggested Uses for the Trains:
I would suggest you keep these near your base, fill a few of the cargo cars with anti air units and fill all the passenger cars up with bazooka or rifle troops. These could help stall an enemy attack, and be useful for a rapid counterattack. The train will destroy anything that strays onto the tracks, so keep your units away from them when possible.

IV: Known glitches and bugs:
By nature, trains are difficult to use. Sometimes cars will split off from the train, or parts of the train will run others over and cause cars to blow up. Other times the train might simply disappear without any reason at all when moving or told to stop.

   The way the minelayer vehicle works, it is possible for it to lay itself off bridges and cliffs without damage to itself, unless it reappears in water, in which case it should sink.
